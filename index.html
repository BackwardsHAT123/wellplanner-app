Well

Name and API number of the well, the coordinate system all coordinates are in, and the surface location: pick a pad slot, or choose a custom location and type X / Y or latitude / longitude. KB elevation = ground elevation + KB above ground; every TVD is measured from KB. Leave VS azimuth blank to use the plan’s own direction (the azimuth of the lateral plane, or surface to target 1).

Well

Plan versions

A well can hold several alternative plans (targets and path design) so you can move targets and compare before choosing one. New version copies the current plan and switches to it; the original stays as it was. Surface location, slot, logs, tops and the actual survey belong to the well, not to a version. ☆ Select marks the version chosen to drill, and Show other versions draws the others as dashed lines on the plots (they are not included in clearance checks).

Pad

▸Pad template

Enter one known surface point, say which slot it is, then set the spacing and rotation.
Click an empty slot to plan a new well there. It copies this well’s design and moves its targets by the same offset as the surface. Click a filled slot to open that well.

▸Lateral spacing

Sets the distance between laterals. The reference well keeps its targets. Every other horizontal well gets a copy of them, moved sideways by a multiple of the spacing, in the same order as the surface locations.
Stagger moves every other lateral (1st, 3rd… from the reference) deeper by that many feet; use a negative number for shallower. This overwrites the target positions of the other wells.

Clearance

Pad wells (and reference wells on the pad) are expected to be closer than the alert distance near surface: they start one slot apart, often 50 ft, and run parallel down the vertical hole. Nothing above the shallower of the two kickoffs counts. From there until the pair first gets farther apart than the alert distance, it only alerts if they close in from their surface spacing by more than the amount above; after that the normal distance and separation-factor limits apply.
Other wells on the pad are included automatically (for those, the alert only counts below the shallower of the two kickoffs, since parallel vertical holes sit one slot apart). The distance alert is center-to-center; the separation factor comes from the simplified survey-uncertainty model below.

▾Survey uncertainty (screening)

Simplified model: random and systematic inclination and azimuth error plus depth scale error, with rounded values for typical tools. It is not an ISCWSA tool-code calculation and the values are not checked against one. Use it to flag wells for a proper anti-collision run, not to replace it. Separation factor = center distance / (2.79 × combined 1-sigma error along the line between the wells).

Drill string

▾Drill string

Listed from the bit up. The last row runs to surface, so its length is not used. Weight is the adjusted weight in air, tool joints included. Torque uses the tool joint OD as the contact radius.

Hole, mud and operating values

Path picks the planned survey or the actual survey (the rest from the plan). The casing shoe (blank = the Landing point) splits cased-hole and open-hole friction. Open hole sensitivity adds dashed lines for other friction factors. Weight and torque on bit are used for drilling and sliding; the tensile and torque limits are drawn on the charts. Tortuosity adds a sine wave to the planned path, which is otherwise too smooth and under-predicts torque and drag.

About the Torque & drag page

  • KPI row: hookloads and torques with the bit at TD, the overpull margin to the tensile limit, and the most weight you can slide with. Red means a limit is broken or the string locks up.
  • The red box lists what would go wrong (lock-up, sliding weight, buckling); the box under it lists the assumptions (casing shoe, buoyancy, tortuosity).
  • Hookload and Surface torque vs bit depth: what you would see at surface as the bit goes down, for each operation. Solid lines use the open-hole friction set in Hole, mud and operating values; dashed lines use the sensitivity values.
  • Axial force and Torque along the string: with the bit at TD, from the bit (bottom) to surface (top). Compression is left of zero; the dashed lines are the sinusoidal and helical buckling limits.
  • Hover any chart to read the values at that MD. With several plan versions, a table compares them; the table at the bottom lists every 500 ft and exports to CSV.
  • The string is set in Drill string and the hole, mud and friction in Hole, mud and operating values on the left.

Every 500 ft of bit depth, open-hole friction
Soft-string model (Johancsik, Friesen and Dawson, 1984): the string has no bending stiffness and lies against the wall, so the contact force comes from its buoyed weight and from axial load carried round the curves. Friction opposes the motion: pulling out (pick-up), running in (slack-off) and sliding (weight on bit applied without rotation). Rotating off bottom has no axial drag. Sinusoidal buckling (Dawson-Paslay; Lubinski in vertical hole) and helical buckling (Chen; Wu in vertical hole) limits are shown, and helically buckled pipe adds wall force of r F²/4EI (sinusoidal r F²/8EI). Surge, swab, viscous drag and stiff-string effects are not modelled. Calibrate the friction factors on offset wells’ pick-up and slack-off weights before relying on the numbers.
